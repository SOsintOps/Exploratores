Exploratores Toolkit: User Guide

Welcome to Exploratores, an OSINT toolkit developed to support the Global Security Team's investigations by providing a curated collection of direct search links and resources.

Getting Started

To effectively utilize the Exploratores toolkit, please consider the following:

  • Prerequisites: For optimal functionality and access to certain resources, ensure you have Tor Browser installed and an active VPN connection. Investigative accounts for various platforms (e.g., Facebook, LinkedIn, Twitter/X) are necessary for many social media and people search tools.
  • Ethical Use: All tools and resources within Exploratores must be used in accordance with applicable laws, regulations, and American Express policies. Conduct all activities with the highest ethical standards.

How to Use Exploratores

  • Navigate: Use the top navigation menu to explore thematic investigative areas. Each menu item typically leads to a page with specialized tools for that category.
  • Input Data: On each tool page, enter your specific queries (names, addresses, emails, keywords, etc.) into the appropriate input fields.
  • Select Tools: Click the buttons corresponding to the specific search engines or resources you wish to query with your input. Searches will generally open in new browser tabs.
  • Analyze & Correlate: The information obtained through these tools always requires careful analysis, correlation with other sources, and critical verification by the analyst. Exploratores facilitates collection, but the analytical rigor is paramount.
  • OPSEC (Operational Security): Always apply sound OPSEC precautions during your investigative activities to protect your identity, location, and the nature of your investigation.

Overview of Tool Categories

The toolkit is organized into several key investigative areas accessible via the navigation menu:

  • Search Engines: General and specialized search engine access.
  • People & Identities: Tools for investigating names, phone numbers, physical addresses, vehicles, usernames, and email addresses.
  • Social Media: Direct links and search functionalities for major social media platforms.
  • Websites: Tools for domain and IP address investigation.
  • GeoInt: Mapping and geolocation tools.
  • Media Analysis: Resources for image, video, and document analysis and search.
  • Business Intelligence: Tools for investigating businesses, government records, and virtual currencies.
  • Auxiliary Tools: Includes CyberChef for data manipulation and these Guidelines.

NOTE: Information sources and online tools can change frequently. If a particular link or tool is broken or outdated, refer to internal documentation or team resources for alternatives. The effectiveness of many tools also depends on the quality of the input data and the analyst's skill in interpreting results.

Global Security Analytic Standards: THE DRAFT FRAMEWORK

Important Disclaimer: The following standards and guidelines are for internal Global Security team reference and are intended to complement, not replace, any official American Express policies, procedures, or established directives. In case of any conflict, official American Express documentation takes precedence.

In conducting all analysis, team members should adhere to the Global Security Analytic Standards outlined below.


Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF): Adherence to the five Core Analytic Standards and nine Analytic Tradecraft Standards outlined below is mandatory for all Global Security intelligence products. These standards are designed to ensure our analysis is consistently objective, rigorous, timely, relevant to stakeholders, and clearly communicates judgments and uncertainties, thereby upholding the integrity and utility of our work.

These Global Security Analytic Standards are internal guidelines that establish the core principles governing the production and evaluation of intelligence products created by our team. They also articulate the responsibility of our analysts to strive for excellence, integrity, and rigor in their analytic thinking and work practices. The standards laid out in these guidelines serve as the core principles of intelligence analysis and are applied in each intelligence product in a manner appropriate to its purpose, the type and scope of its underlying source information, its production timeline, and its internal stakeholders. Furthermore, these standards are the foundational assessment criteria for any program of review of our intelligence products. Each Global Security team member must adhere to these standards in their evaluations and production of intelligence. This means that every intelligence product created by a Global Security team member must adhere to these criteria to ensure quality and reliability.

Let's explore these standards in detail:

The Five Analytic Standards

These are the fundamental principles guiding analysis within Global Security:

Objective: Analysts must perform their functions with objectivity and be aware of their own assumptions and reasoning. They are required to employ reasoning techniques and practical mechanisms to reveal and mitigate bias. While analysts can be influenced by existing analytic positions, they must consider alternative perspectives and contrary information. Analysis should not be unduly constrained by previous judgments when new developments indicate a modification is necessary.

Independent of political consideration: Analytic assessments must not be distorted by, nor shaped for, advocacy of a particular audience, agenda, or policy viewpoint. Crucially, analytic judgments must not be influenced by the force of preference for a particular policy.

Timely: Analysis must be disseminated in time for it to be actionable by stakeholders. Our analytic elements have the responsibility to be continually aware of events of intelligence interest, stakeholder activities and schedules, and intelligence requirements and priorities, in order to provide useful analysis at the right time.

Based on all available sources of intelligence information: Analysis should be informed by all relevant available information. Our analytic elements should identify and address critical information gaps and work with collection activities and data providers to develop access and collection strategies.

Implements and exhibits Analytic Tradecraft Standards: This standard serves as an overarching requirement, mandating adherence to the nine specific Analytic Tradecraft Standards outlined below.

The Nine Analytic Tradecraft Standards

These standards provide specific guidance on the techniques and practices analysts must follow to produce rigorous and credible analysis. Adherence to these is essential under the fifth Analytic Standard.

1. Properly describes quality and credibility of underlying sources, data, and methodologies: Our intelligence products should identify underlying sources and methodologies upon which judgments are based. It is necessary to describe factors affecting source quality and credibility, such as accuracy, completeness, potential denial and deception, and the currency of information. Source summary statements should be strongly encouraged and used to provide a holistic assessment of the strengths or weaknesses in the source base.

2. Properly expresses and explains uncertainties associated with major analytic judgments: Our intelligence products should indicate and explain the basis for uncertainties associated with major analytic judgments. This specifically involves articulating the likelihood of an event or development and the analyst's confidence in the basis for this judgment. Degrees of likelihood encompass a full spectrum from remote to nearly certain. Analysis confidence should be based on the logic and evidentiary base, including the quantity and quality of source material and the understanding of the topic. Products should note sources of uncertainty and how uncertainties affect analysis. Appropriate products should identify indicators that would alter the likelihood of uncertainty or major analytic judgments. Consistency in terminology and logical advanced is critical. For expressions of likelihood or probability, an analytic product must use standardized sets of terms, such as the following examples:

Almost No Chance Very Unlikely Unlikely Roughly Even Chance Likely Very Likely Almost Certain(ly)
Remote Highly Improbable (Improbably) Improbable (Improbably) Roughly Even Odds Probable (Probably) Highly Probable Nearly Certain
01-05% 05-20% 20-45% 45-55% 55-80% 80-95% 95-99%

Analysts are strongly encouraged not to mix terms from different approved sets. To avoid confusion, products expressing confidence levels (e.g., "high confidence") must not combine a confidence level and a degree of likelihood in the same sentence.

3. Properly distinguishes between underlying intelligence information and analysts’ assumptions and judgments: Our intelligence products should clearly distinguish statements that convey underlying intelligence information used in analysis from statements that convey assumptions or judgments. Assumptions are defined as suppositions used to frame or support an argument; judgments affect analytic interpretation of underlying intelligence information. Judgments are defined as conclusions based on underlying intelligence information, analysis, and assumptions. Products should state assumptions explicitly when they serve as the linchpin of an argument or when they bridge key information gaps. Products should explain the implications for judgments if assumptions prove to be incorrect. Products also should identify indicators that, if detected, would alter judgments.

4. Incorporates analysis of alternatives: Analysis of alternatives is the systematic evaluation of differing hypotheses to explain events or phenomena, explore near-term outcomes, and imagine possible futures to mitigate surprise and risk. This is particularly important when major judgments are associated with significant uncertainties or complexity. When probability events could produce high-impact results, addressing alternatives is crucial. Products should address factors such as assumptions, likelihood, or implications related to our organization's interests. Products also should identify indicators that, if detected, would affect the likelihood of identified alternatives.

5. Demonstrates stakeholder relevance and addresses implications: Our intelligence products should provide information and insight on issues relevant to our internal stakeholders and address the implications of the information and analysis they provide. Products should add value by addressing prospects, contexts, threats, or factors offering opportunities for action.

6. Uses clear and logical argumentation: Our intelligence products should present a clear main analytic message up front. If there are multiple judgments, the main message should be drawn collectively from them. All analytic judgments should be effectively supported by relevant intelligence information and coherent reasoning. Language and syntax should convey meaning unambiguously. Products should be internally consistent and acknowledge significant supporting and contrary information affecting judgments.

7. Explicitly explains change to or consistency of analytic judgments: Our intelligence products should state how their major judgments on a topic are consistent with or represent a change from those in previously published analysis, or represent initial coverage of a topic. Products need not be lengthy in explaining this but should avoid boilerplate language. Recurrent products like daily crisis reports should note any changes in judgments; absent changes, they don't need to confirm consistency with previous editions. Significant differences in analytic judgment between different analytic elements or teams should be fully considered and brought to the attention of stakeholders.

8. Makes accurate judgments and assessments: Our intelligence products should apply expertise and logic to make the most accurate judgments and assessments possible, based on the information available and known information gaps. They should present all judgments useful to stakeholders and should not avoid difficult judgments to minimise the risk of being wrong. The analytic message a stakeholder receives should be the one the analyst intended to send. Products should express judgments as clearly and precisely as possible, reducing ambiguity by addressing the likelihood, nature, and source of the outcome or development. Clarity permits assessment for accuracy when an evaluation is available.

9. Incorporates effective visual information where appropriate: Our intelligence products should incorporate visual information to clarify an analytic message and to complement or enhance the presentation of data and analysis. Visual presentations should be used when information or concepts (e.g., spatial or temporal relationships) can be conveyed better graphically. This can include tables, flow charts, or images. Visual information should always be clear and pertinent. Analytic content in visual information should also adhere to other tradecraft standards.

By adhering to these five Analytic Standards and the nine Analytic Tradecraft Standards, our intelligence products aim to be objective, rigorous, well-supported, clear, relevant, and accurately convey the state of available information and associated uncertainties. This comprehensive framework is fundamental to maintaining the quality and integrity of analysis across the Global Security team.
